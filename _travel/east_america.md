**2026.07.22 – 2026.07.29 Location: Philadelphia, USA**

I was here for a conference — ICM, lots of talks, lots of coffee.  

Between talks and handshakes, I escaped to the museums. The Art Museum — those grand steps, Rocky's ghost still jogging up them — but inside, the real magic was the Impressionist rooms. I stood in front of a Renoir (my favorite artist) for what felt like an hour — faces and flesh, rosy-cheeked women in boater hats. His figures seemed to melt into the warm air, their skin glowing like they'd just stepped out of the sun. My favorite painting is the following one:

![](/images/memories/Phil_art_museum.jpg)
{: .article-image}

A tangle of bodies and fabric and Alpine grass, all dappled in that golden, filtered light that Sargent painted like he was chasing a dream. The figures are lounging — languid, lazy, like they've been there all afternoon and plan to stay forever. You can almost feel the heat of the Italian summer, the weight of the parasols, the way the brushstrokes get thick and lush where the sun hits a sleeve or a cheek.

Then I walked to the **Rodin Museum**, smaller, quieter, tucked behind a garden. Outside, an old man with a weathered cap was pointing at The Gates of Hell, gesturing, muttering something about *“the struggle, the beauty, the door that never opens.”* I stood there with him for a minute, watching the little figures twist in bronze.

I also discovered gelato. Not a cone, not a cup — a BOX! 

![](/images/memories/Philly.jpg)

**2026.07.29 – 2026.07.31 Location: Washington DC, USA**

The train from Philly to DC was short, but it felt like sliding into a different chapter — the sky clearer, the buildings more serious, like the whole city had its shoulders back.

A 16-oz ribeye steak at Old Ebbitt Grill sent me into a glorious protein coma, wiping out my evening. The next day I walked over 20km to atone.

Under the freedom dome at the Capitol, a shaft of sunlight broke through the oculus and fell square on the equestrian portrait of Lincoln in the corridor. It wasn't staged — just light and angle and luck — but for a moment, the whole building felt like a living photograph.

**2026.07.31 – 2026.08.04 Location: NYC, USA**

Last night in NYC - Sunset Park.

Sunlight glittered on a little white dog, and people sprawled on the grass like they were being baked through. Hard to believe that rain poured down this morning.

And the park filled with old Chinese songs, as a few people started doing tai chi and square dancing, right there on the Brooklyn hillside, arms swaying, feet stepping in rhythm to a melody that felt both foreign and deeply familiar. 

The sky did its thing — blue fading to peach, peach bleeding into lavender, the water of the harbour shimmering like crumpled tinfoil. Manhattan sat across the bay, tiny and sharp.

Manhattan still feels like magic - second time here, still believing 'Every man gets his wish.' here.
